Project Ironclad — Substation Modernization Program
(FICTIONAL TEST DATA — Tenant A)

Summary
Project Ironclad is Northwind Grid Authority's flagship capital program to modernize aging
500 kV and 230 kV substation equipment across the Cascadia North region. The program runs
from fiscal year 1 through fiscal year 5 and is sponsored by Chief Reliability Engineer
Dana Holloway.

Objectives
1. Replace end-of-life oil-filled circuit breakers at Mistral Substation with modern
   SF6-free vacuum interrupters.
2. Deploy digital protection relays and IEC 61850 process-bus communications at the eight
   highest-risk substations.
3. Add redundant control and protection at the Aurora Interconnect terminals to eliminate
   single points of failure.

Phases
- Phase 1 (Mistral Substation): full breaker replacement and relay upgrade. This is the
  anchor site for Project Ironclad and the proving ground for the digital protection design.
- Phase 2 (Highlands corridor): protection upgrades on the Aurora Interconnect terminals.
- Phase 3 (Coast corridor): substation automation at the coastal wind-import stations.

Budget and schedule
The program carries a fictional capital budget of 410 million credits. Phase 1 at Mistral
Substation is scheduled for an 18-month outage-coordinated rollout, sequenced around
Cascadia North Core peak-load seasons to avoid reliability impacts.

Risks
- Long-lead procurement for 500 kV breakers.
- Outage coordination on the Aurora Interconnect, which carries critical Highlands hydro.
- Workforce availability for IEC 61850 commissioning.

Expected outcome
On completion, Project Ironclad is expected to reduce forced-outage rates at Mistral
Substation by an estimated 60 percent and bring the Aurora Interconnect to N-1-1 protection
redundancy. All details are fictional and used only for isolation testing.
