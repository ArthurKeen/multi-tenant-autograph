Northwind Grid Authority — Organizational Overview
(FICTIONAL TEST DATA — Tenant A)

Northwind Grid Authority (NGA) is a fictional independent transmission system operator
responsible for planning and operating the high-voltage electricity backbone of the
Cascadia North region. NGA does not generate or retail electricity; it coordinates the
reliable bulk transfer of power across 12,400 kilometers of transmission lines and 84
substations on behalf of connected generators and distribution utilities.

Mission
NGA's mandate is to keep the Cascadia North grid reliable, balanced, and open to fair
access. The organization maintains the regional transmission plan, administers
interconnection requests, and operates the real-time control center in the city of
Harbormere.

Operating regions
- Cascadia North Core: dense urban load centers around Harbormere and Fort Lindel.
- Cascadia North Highlands: mountainous corridors carrying hydro power south.
- Cascadia North Coast: coastal corridors integrating offshore and onshore wind imports.

Key assets
- Mistral Substation: NGA's largest 500 kV switching station, located outside Fort Lindel.
- Aurora Interconnect: the 500 kV double-circuit line linking the Highlands hydro fleet to
  the Core load center.
- Harbormere Control Center: the 24/7 balancing-authority operations hub.

Leadership
The grid operations group is led by Chief Reliability Engineer Dana Holloway, who oversees
transmission planning, the interconnection queue, and major capital programs such as the
Project Ironclad substation modernization initiative.

Governance
NGA operates under a regional reliability charter and reports reliability metrics quarterly
to the Cascadia North Reliability Council. All figures, names, and projects in this document
are fictional and exist only to test multi-tenant data isolation.
